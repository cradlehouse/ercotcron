# Shadowprice — CRR Valuation Methodology

**Version 1.2 — September 2026**
**Instrument scope:** ERCOT Congestion Revenue Rights (Options and Obligations, all Time-of-Use blocks, monthly and long-term tenors)
**Purpose:** Independent, reproducible marks for CRR positions, suitable for collateral assessment, portfolio wind-down, and transaction pricing. Marks are **not** fair-value measurements for financial reporting, audit evidence, or financial-reporting support, and may not be represented as such (see Terms of Service §3).

This document describes what the mark is, every input that produces it, the controls around it, and — deliberately, at equal length — what the mark is *not* and where it can be wrong. A valuation methodology that hides its limitations is not audit-grade.

---

## 1. What is being valued

A CRR entitles its holder to day-ahead market (DAM) congestion payments on a defined path for a defined month:

- **Option (OPT):** pays `max(0, sink price − source price)` per MWh, per hour in its Time-of-Use block. Downside floored at zero.
- **Obligation (OBL):** pays `(sink price − source price)` signed. The holder **pays** when the spread inverts. Obligations are marked with the unfloored, signed payoff.

Time-of-Use blocks partition the month's hours into PeakWD (hours-ending 7–22, NERC weekdays), PeakWE (hours-ending 7–22, weekends and NERC holidays), and Off-peak (all remaining hours). A position's monthly value is its per-MWh rate × MW × the exact hour count of its block in its delivery month. Hour counts are computed from the calendar, including NERC holiday treatment (e.g., September 2026: PeakWD 336 h, PeakWE 144 h, Off-peak 240 h, with Labor Day classified as weekend).

## 2. Data sources

Every input is public and independently obtainable. No client-supplied or confidential data enters the mark.

| Input | Source | Coverage held |
|---|---|---|
| DAM settlement point prices (hourly, nodal) | ERCOT NP4-190/NP4-183 via public API | 2+ years, all settlement points |
| CRR auction awards & clearing prices | ERCOT CRR Market Results (public MIS) | 48 auctions: monthly + all long-term sequences |
| DAM binding constraints & shadow prices | ERCOT NP4-191 | 2 years (~718k constraint-hours) |
| RUC-committed constraints | ERCOT NP5-755 | 1+ year (presence only; the report carries no shadow price) |
| Unplanned resource outages | ERCOT NP1-346-ER | 3.7 years (published on a 3-day lag) |

All raw ingests are timestamped on receipt (`ingested_at`) and retained; marks can be recomputed as-of any historical ingest state.

## 3. Valuation model

The mark is **realized-payoff based**, not model-forecast based. For a path/TOU/hedge-type position deliverable in calendar month *M*:

1. Compute the hourly payoff series for the path from DAM settlement prices over the full history held.
2. **Calendar-month conditioning:** the per-MWh rate is the mean realized payoff of the path's TOU block in prior occurrences of calendar month *M* (e.g., a September position is priced off Septembers). Congestion is strongly seasonal; a 12-month blend lets a single extreme January contaminate a September mark.
3. Where fewer than two prior occurrences of month *M* exist, fall back to the median across all months, and flag the mark as `LOW_HISTORY`.
4. Monthly value = rate × MW × exact TOU hours in the delivery month. Multi-month strips are the sum of their months, each conditioned on its own calendar month.

**Auction cross-reference.** Each mark is reported alongside the path's recent auction clearing basis: the average clearing price across the last three monthly auctions in which the path traded. Clearing prices are taken from monthly auctions only — long-term auction clears embed different capacity-release percentages and are not comparable. This gives the user two independent reference points: what the path *pays* (realized) and what the market last *paid for it* (cleared).

## 4. Adjustments

Applied multiplicatively, floored so that total trim never exceeds 75%:

| Trim | Trigger | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Stale driver | The constraint(s) historically driving the path's value have been re-rated or structurally changed within 90 days (see §5) | −30% |
| Thin history | Fewer than 1,500 payoff-hours behind the rate | −20% |
| Spike concentration | Mean payoff > 3× median payoff (value came from a few extreme hours) | −25% |
| Fading congestion | Recent 3-month realized rate < 30% of the historical typical rate | mark uses `min(typical, recent)` |

Every applied trim is disclosed on the mark line. An untrimmed number is never silently substituted.

## 5. Staleness controls (constraint layer)

CRR value is generated by specific transmission constraints. We maintain:

- **A constraint registry:** every constraint observed binding in 2 years of DAM data, tiered by evidential weight (promotable ≥ ~100 binding hours — a threshold measured empirically, not assumed; provisional ≥ 50; candidate below). Constraints silent for 60+ days are flagged *possibly retired*.
- **A novelty detector:** weekly-median constraint limits are monitored for >5% step changes, which indicate re-rates, topology changes, or new equipment. As of this writing, roughly a third of the actively-binding set shows a re-rate within 90 days.
- **An empirical exposure map:** per-node sensitivity (regression slope) of nodal basis to each constraint's shadow price, which links each path to its driving constraints.

A path whose value history predates a structural change in its driving constraint is priced off a grid that no longer exists. This is the single largest source of quiet mispricing in held CRR books and is why the stale-driver trim exists.

## 6. Validation

Standing rule: **the most recent two months of data are always held out** — nothing is fitted or calibrated on them; they are used only to test.

- **Directional reliability (exposure map):** out-of-sample sign agreement between predicted and realized basis response is 96% overall; 97.3% on material exposures vs 93.4% for a random-node control; 97.9% when economically weighted (vs 87.5% control). Direction is trustworthy.
- **Magnitude reliability:** only 60.6% of out-of-sample responses fall within 2× of the predicted magnitude. **Magnitude is not trustworthy** and is never used for position sizing or point-estimate certainty. Marks are therefore anchored on realized payoffs (§3), with the constraint layer used for *flags and trims*, not for magnitudes.
- **Settlement reconciliation (ground truth):** the engine's computed payoffs were reconciled against a cooperating account holder's actual ERCOT settlement statements — 2,327 positions spanning 2024–2027. Of 602 positions whose delivery months fall inside the price archive, **548 (91%) reproduce the statement revenue within 2%**; the remainder deviate by 2–7%, consistent with time-of-use hour-classification edge cases (holiday treatment) rather than price errors. The engine computes value from the same DAM prices ERCOT settles on, and this test confirms it.
- **Predictive-rule graveyard:** seven-plus predictive trading rules (constraint-conditioned DART, and others) were tested walk-forward on expanding windows and failed out-of-sample. They are recorded in an append-only negative-knowledge register and are excluded from the methodology. The mark contains **no forecast alpha** — by policy, not by omission.

## 7. What the mark is not

- **Not a forecast.** It states what a position's delivery months have historically paid and what the market recently cleared it at. It cannot promise the next month resembles history.
- **Not a liquidation price.** There is no active secondary market in ERCOT CRRs; a forced sale may realize substantially less than the mark.
- **Not a sizing tool.** Magnitude uncertainty (§6) makes the constraint layer suitable for direction and staleness detection only.
- **Coverage caveats.** PCRRs, paths with no auction history, and positions whose delivery month has no prior calendar occurrence in the data are marked with reduced-confidence flags rather than excluded silently.

## 8. Data lineage and audit trail

Every published mark must be reproducible and attributable. The system self-audits as follows:

1. **Immutable mark runs.** Each valuation run writes an append-only `mark_runs` record: run ID, UTC timestamp, engine version (git commit SHA), methodology version (this document's version), and **input watermarks** — for each source table, the max `ingested_at` and row count at run time. A mark is meaningless without knowing what data existed when it was struck; the watermark pins that.
2. **Immutable marks.** Individual position marks are written append-only, keyed to their run ID, with the rate, hours, trims applied (each with its trigger value), fallbacks used, and confidence flags. Corrections are issued as new runs — **no mark is ever edited or deleted.**
3. **Input integrity.** Raw ERCOT files are retained as received; a content hash per source file allows any mark to be traced to the exact upstream publications behind it.
4. **Negative knowledge is append-only.** Failed methods and known pitfalls are recorded in a register that only grows; a method removed from the methodology stays visible in the record with the reason.
5. **Sign-off field.** Each run carries a `published_by` and optional `reviewed_by`; marks distributed to a client reference a specific run ID, so any delivered PDF/CSV is traceable to one immutable run.

## 9. Governance and versioning

- This document is versioned; any change to model logic, trims, or thresholds increments the version and is effective only for subsequent runs.
- Threshold changes (e.g., the 90-day staleness window, trim percentages) require a recorded rationale in the change log below.
- The two-month holdout rule (§6) is standing and applies to any future methodology change: a proposed change must not be evaluated on data it was tuned on.

## 10. Scorecard publication rules (pre-registered)

These rules are fixed BEFORE the first Scorecard issue and apply to every
issue. Changing any of them increments this document's version and applies
only to later issues.

**Aggregation floor.** No published statistic describes a cohort of fewer
than 5 distinct account holders, and no single holder may account for more
than 40% of a published bucket's MW. Buckets failing either test are merged
or withheld. The unit of analysis is always the path, price band, or
constraint — never the holder; no holder is ever named or identifiable.

**Self-scoring rules (fixed).** (a) A paper bid "fills" when its limit is at
or above the auction's posted clearing price for the identical path, TOU
block, and hedge type; paper fill size is capped at the MW the auction
actually awarded on that path. (b) Profit and loss is computed only after
the delivery month has fully settled, over the whole month, from the same
DAM prices ERCOT settles on. (c) Realized value is computed for EVERY stored
bid — fills, misses, and non-trades alike — so passed-up wins and dodged
losses are both on the record. (d) Bids are stored append-only before
results post; nothing is added, removed, or edited afterward. A correction
is a new run published alongside the old one.

**Every published figure carries** its sample size, data window, class
definition, and the immutable run ID it derives from.

**Both directions, always.** Favorable and unfavorable results appear with
equal prominence. An issue with no unfavorable result must say why.

**Hypothetical-performance legend.** Every self-scored publication carries
the disclosure that results are hypothetical, no actual bids were submitted,
and no representation is made that any account will achieve similar results
(CFTC Rule 4.41(b) form, used whether or not formally required).

**Historical only.** Scorecard content is historical description. It
contains no forecasts, no forward-looking statements, and no recommendation
to buy, sell, or bid anything.
