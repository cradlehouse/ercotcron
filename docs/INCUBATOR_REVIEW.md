===== VIEWPOINT: market-structure-veteran =====
**1) Is the thesis sound?**

Partially — but it misreads what a CRR "mark" is and who needs one. Bloomberg bond marks and cat-bond dealer sheets aggregate *transaction/quote* information nobody can compute alone. A CRR mark is different: the payoff is deterministic public arithmetic (DAM congestion × MW), and the forward component is a congestion *forecast* — which your own results say is unforecastable in magnitude (60.6% within 2×; seven walk-forward rules, all failed). So the product is an attested backward-looking valuation plus a forward number you've disclaimed for sizing. That's an attestation service, not a price.

Second misread: ERCOT already has a de facto mark — the monthly auction. Every position can be re-offered into it; clearing prices are the live, executable reference desks actually use. "No marks between auctions" overstates the gap for the people who dominate holdings (financial players, who self-mark and will not outsource valuation absent compulsion — and ERCOT, being non-FERC, has no compulsion). Third: the collateral-relief exit motive doesn't route through you. TPES is ERCOT's formula; a Shadowprice mark changes no holder's collateral. Sellers relieve collateral by selling into the monthly auction, today, without you. Fourth: publishing a target list of stale-flagged holders, when award data names everyone, will read to holders as arming predators against them — expect hostility, not conversion, from a chunk of the 285.

The seat is empty partly because nobody needs to sit in it. The genuine buyers are the minority with fair-value/audit obligations (LSEs, munis, banks, funds with LP reporting) and entities in wind-down.

**2) Novel vs. known**

- **Price-bucket curve / winner's curse**: known on desks. Cheap-option lottery underpricing and expensive-path overbidding is documented in FTR academic literature and visible in IMM annuals. Novelty = monthly cadence and 588k-position granularity, not the finding.
- **OPT −7.6% vs OBL +22.3%**: directionally known — every experienced participant knows the option floor is systematically overpaid. The *magnitude*, published, at monthly cadence, is genuinely new in ERCOT and will get quoted.
- **Settlement validation (91% within 2%)**: internally, this is table stakes — every back office reconciles settlements. As a *published external attestation* it's novel and is your single strongest credibility asset, though it currently proves arithmetic replication, not valuation skill. One holder ≠ validation; say so before someone else does.

**3) Best business, from market structure**

Not the venue (your $0.1–1.5M ceiling math is right) and not marks-as-benchmark (no natural payer at scale). The structure points to **valuation-for-purpose concierge**: attested audit letters for the fair-value-obligated minority, wind-down/estate mandates at %-of-notional (Steve is the prototype — 13-year books retiring, 285 holders, some fraction exiting every year), and litigation/expert work. Episodic, rich, and the append-only audit trail is exactly what those buyers pay for. Fund it with the Scorecard as marketing — the docs' own conclusion, correctly held — but pair it with the one *recurring* product desks actually buy: workflow (bid sheets, constraint-change feed) at $250–1k/mo. Desks pay for tools that touch the auction deadline, not for numbers about last month. The 90-day plan is right; just aim Layer 2 outreach at CFOs and auditors, not traders, and get CFTC counsel before anything touches a transfer.
