## 1. Framing

Both reviews agree this is not primarily a securities-law business. The live
risk surfaces are, in order:

1. Uniform auction limits distributed to horizontal competitors (antitrust /
   PUCT market-conduct).
2. Personalized outputs (allocator, holder views) — liability and reliance,
   not registration.
3. Misleading presentation of accuracy or track record (CEA anti-fraud, Texas
   DTPA).
4. Contract liability with no terms of service in place.
5. Third-party reliance on paid valuations (Restatement §552).

Securities law proper (Texas Securities Act, Advisers Act) does not reach
CRRs. Do not spend counsel time there.

## 2. Regulatory baseline (Confirm)

- The CFTC RTO/ISO exemption order (2013; reaffirmed 2020) covers
  ERCOT/PUCT-supervised CRRs.
- The order exempts the transactions and "any person … rendering advice or
  rendering other services with respect to" them. Working assumption: CTA
  registration is not required for advice about ERCOT CRRs. Confirm the exact
  coverage language and conditions.
- The order preserves anti-fraud and anti-manipulation authority: CEA
  §6(c)(1), Rule 180.1, §9(a)(2) false market reports, and §4o (CTA
  anti-fraud, applies whether or not registered). Operate as if all four
  apply to every publication.
- Aspire Commodities v. GDF Suez (5th Cir. 2016): the exemption cuts off the
  CEA §22 private right of action. CEA exposure is CFTC-enforcement only;
  subscriber claims come under contract, negligence, DTPA, and §552 instead.
- Texas: PURA §39.157 and 16 TAC §25.503 (wholesale market conduct —
  collusion, coordinated bidding) apply to ERCOT participants. ERCOT IMM
  (Potomac Economics) monitors CRR auctions.

## 3. Findings by question

### Q1 — Scorecard issue #1

Both reviews: publishable in principle. Aggregate, reproducible,
methodology-bound statistics on public data are far from the scienter both
§9(a)(2) and Rule 180.1 require. The risk is misleading presentation, not
being wrong. Resolution: publish with changes. Required: minimum-n rule per
bucket; pre-registered scoring rules; sample size, window, class definition,
and run ID on every figure; favorable and unfavorable results shown alike;
Rule 4.41(b) hypothetical-performance legend on self-scored sheets (they were
not traded); hard separation of historical statistics from anything
forward-looking; site copy corrected so the ~96% sign-accuracy figure is
never presented as bid-sheet accuracy. Texas exposure: defamation / business
disparagement only if a statistic maps to an identifiable holder. Minimum-n
rule closes this.

### Q2 — Bid-sheet liability and terms

Both reviews: clickwrap before the first charge. Disclaimers describe; they
do not cap. Resolution: terms must include no-reliance and sophistication
acknowledgments, no fiduciary/advisory relationship, subscriber sole
responsibility for bidding decisions, warranty disclaimer,
consequential-damages exclusion, liability cap at fees paid in prior 12
months, indemnity for subscriber's own trading, Texas law with Dallas County
venue or arbitration, and stored acceptance evidence. Cap and negligence
language must be conspicuous (Texas fair-notice / express-negligence
doctrine). Do not attempt a DTPA waiver; rely on business-consumer
exemptions. Terms do not protect fraud or intentional misconduct. Copy:
"enter the shown price and never more" is an instruction. Replace with
"reference limit price" plus a range or confidence band. This one change
helps Q2, Q5, and Q6 at once.

### Q3 — Episodic valuations (§552)

Both reviews: highest-liability tier in the business. Texas follows
Restatement §552 (McCamish); liability runs to the limited group known to
rely. Resolution: tailored engagement letter for every engagement — named
sole intended user; purpose and data cutoff; statement that the work is
arithmetic on settlement statements, not an audit, fairness opinion, forward
valuation, or financial-reporting attestation; third-party reliance
prohibited absent a separate reliance letter; client representation that
supplied data is complete; liability cap at a multiple of fees; prompt
claim-notice requirement; right to withdraw the report if data proves
incomplete. E&O in force before the first engagement (claims-made;
retroactive date matters). Broker prices limits against plausible reliance
exposure, not current revenue; $1M/$1M is the floor a counterparty will
accept.

### Q4 — Credit-side reports to banks

Both reviews: yellow. Public source data and a published rubric support the
rating-agency analogy (opinion on disclosed facts), but do not eliminate
defamation, disparagement, or negligent-misstatement risk. Resolution: entity
counterparties and commercial-credit uses only (individual traders in the
CRRAH registry could pull the product toward FCRA); counterparty receives the
report and a fixed comment/correction window; every report stamped with run
ID and a statement that it reflects only public CRR awards and nothing about
hedges, collateral, or other books; no implication of a credit rating,
default probability, or verified financial condition. Media/publisher's
liability coverage is the policy that answers this lane.

### Q5 — Publisher posture, allocator, holder views

Divergence: Review 1 treated CTA status as the central question and proposed
a subscriber-controlled formula for the allocator. Review 2 identified the
"rendering advice" relief in the CFTC order, which moves the question from
registration to liability, and recommended the allocator be descriptive only.
Resolution: adopt Review 2's stricter line. Ship the bid sheet without a
quantity-output allocator at launch. Any position-specific tool is
descriptive only — no buy/sell/hold, no bid limit, no size — until terms are
live and counsel has reviewed the real facts. Holder views stay: the grade on
a path is identical for every holder of that path; the view only filters
public data by owner. State that in terms; never add holder-specific
adjustments. Standing rule: no human at Shadowprice communicates about a
specific subscriber's positions.

### Q6 — Uniform limits to horizontal competitors

Both reviews: the strongest concern in the brief. Not automatically unlawful
— Sherman §1 and PUCT §25.503 require an agreement — but a vendor
distributing identical prices to competitors with an instruction to follow
them is the DOJ RealPage fact pattern, and clustered bids are uncomfortable
evidence. Resolution: never ingest subscribers' private bids, positions, or
intentions — public data only, stated in terms; imperative language removed;
no features revealing other subscribers' identities, bids, or behavior; no
forums or customer-to-customer channels; antitrust clause in terms confirming
independent bidding; internal record that model outputs are generated
independently of subscriber data; consider a courtesy briefing to the ERCOT
IMM once paying users exist. Subscriber mix does not need managing if these
hold.

### Q7 — Outreach

Both reviews: cold B2B email is lawful under CAN-SPAM with truthful headers
and subject, ad identification, physical postal address, and a working
opt-out honored within ten business days. TCPA out of scope absent calls or
texts to mobile numbers. Additional: harvesting trap — 15 U.S.C. §7704(b)(1)
aggravates violations when addresses are pulled from a site whose terms
prohibit it. Read ERCOT's website terms and the CRRAH registry's stated
purpose before pulling contacts. The privacy-policy clause is irrelevant to
non-users; US law needs no consent basis for B2B email. Texas TDPSA exempts
SBA-size small businesses.

### Q8 — Not asked

- Entity formation before the first charge; every cap above is decorative
  without it.
- ERCOT data-use terms: confirm no redistribution limits on awards and
  settlement data.
- Design partner receives sheets at the identical timestamp as subscribers,
  documented.
- Anchor methodology versions externally (published hashes or signed tags) so
  append-only is not only the database's word.
- Insurance ask is E&O + media liability + cyber, not E&O alone.

## 4b. Implementation notes (2026-09-05)

- A11 audit: subscriber-linked tables are `user_holders` (claimed CRR codes —
  public identifiers + verification status), `profiles` (plan/trial),
  `watchlists` (alert configuration, empty; must never feed valuations and is
  never visible to other users), `holder_verifications` (tokens),
  `credit_grants`/`access_log` (dormant credit lane). No table stores any
  subscriber's bids, positions, or intentions. The design partner's workbook
  bid prices in `path_valuations.bid_price` are consented validation data,
  displayed only to the verified holder, and are not a model input.
- A10 goes further than the earlier mitigation (equal-split relabel): the
  budget-to-MW tool is gone from the build. Methodology v1.2 records both.
- Valuations are locked behind authenticated-only access as of 2026-09-04;
  the model pipeline reads only ERCOT-published data (supports A13's record).

- A16 findings (ERCOT Terms of Use, ercot.com/help/terms, read 2026-09-05):
  (1) REDISTRIBUTION — expressly permitted: "Raw data provided in public
  portions of this website may be used, reproduced, and redistributed in
  compilations, charts, and analyses" (ERCOT disclaims accuracy of downstream
  compilations; our own accuracy disclosures already cover this). The core
  business model is within the written terms. (2) EMAIL — §8: "Use of ERCOT
  list Server (email) list for marketing products and/or services is strictly
  prohibited." This targets ERCOT's listserv distribution lists; the CRRAH
  registry is a posted market report, not the list server — but it is close
  enough to the §7704(b)(1) harvesting aggravator that counsel should confirm
  before any cold send (folded into counsel question 8). (3) No explicit
  scraping/crawling prohibition; only a bar on activity degrading site
  performance — our ingestion honors rate limits (Retry-After respected in
  code). API use is additionally governed by the Data Portal terms (usage
  limits; no circumvention) which we comply with.
